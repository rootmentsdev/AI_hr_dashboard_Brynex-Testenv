1. Product Objective
The HR Brain should not be designed as a generic HR chatbot. It should function as an intelligence
and decision-support layer over workforce data, business performance and management workflows.
The product should answer three classes of questions:
 • What is happening in the workforce?
 • Why is it happening?
 • What management action is most likely to improve the result?
 The system must combine company-specific data with structured management
logic, while keeping humans responsible for consequential employment decisions.

3. Core Data Object: Employee Digital Profile
Every employee must have a continuously evolving digital profile. This becomes the core right-brain
HR entity and the common key that links HR, finance, sales and operations data.
Data Group
Examples

Data-design rule
The profile should store both master data and time-series events. Do not overwrite important
historical states such as role, salary, manager, branch or KPI target; preserve effective dates so AI
can reason over change.

7. Onboarding & Probation

10. AI Performance Diagnosis
The system should never equate a low result directly with a poor employee. It should diagnose
context and possible root causes before recommending action.
Signal
Diagnostic Questions
Performance decline
Attendance issue? Low traffic? Bad shift? Stock issue? Training gap? Manager problem?
Target unrealistic?
High payroll ratio
Revenue decline? Excess staffing? Overtime? Shift overlap? Low productivity?
High attrition
Specific manager? Role? tenure band? location? pay structure? onboarding weakness?
Low conversion
Individual issue? lead quality? product availability? traffic mix? training? pricing?
Expected output
For each significant issue: evidence summary, likely causes, confidence level, missing data, 2-3
management options, expected impact, risks and recommended next action.

13. Engagement, Retention & Employee
Relations
The system can use operational signals to surface workforce risk, but consequential actions must
remain human-reviewed.
 • Attendance deterioration
 • Repeated leave or shift conflicts
 • Manager changes
 • Performance decline
 • Training disengagement
 • Tenure-based attrition patterns
 • Documented employee feedback
 • Recognition frequency
 • Role / location mismatch
 
Safeguard
Do not use sensitive or protected characteristics to score employee quality, retention risk or
suitability. AI-generated risk indicators should trigger review, not automatic promotion, discipline,
termination or compensation decisions.

16. Standard Management Decision Workflow
Data → Signal → Variance → Diagnosis → Root Cause → Options → Expected Impact →
Risk → Human Decision → Action Plan → Owner → Deadline → Result → Learning
Step
Example
Signal
Payroll ratio reached 18% vs 12% target
Diagnosis
Revenue down, shift overlap high, overtime concentrated in two teams
Option A
Improve productivity without headcount reduction
Option B
Transfer excess capacity to understaffed branches
Option C
Roster optimisation and reduced overlap
Decision
Management selects B + C
30-day outcome
Payroll ratio improves to 14.5%; revenue maintained
Learning
Store staffing benchmark and roster rule updated

Prioritise data relationships over collecting hundreds of disconnected fields.
Employee → Role → Branch → Manager → Salary Cost → Attendance / Hours →
Activities → Target → Actual Output → Performance → Training → Management
Action → Outcome
This creates the key business relationship: Cost → Activity → Output → Outcome.

20. Access, Governance & Audit Requirements
 • Role-based access by company, brand, branch and department
 • Separate employee self-service, manager, HR, finance and executive permissions
 • Field-level protection for compensation, disciplinary and personal data
 • Complete audit log for data changes, recommendations, approvals and AI actions
 • Source traceability for every AI conclusion where possible
 • Human approval for high-impact employment actions
 • Configurable data-retention and deletion policies
 • Tenant isolation between customer companies

23. Development Principles
 • Design for structured data first; use documents and chat as supporting interfaces.
 • Keep historical states with effective dates so trends and cause/effect can be analysed.
 • Every AI insight should identify the data used, missing data and confidence.
 • Separate raw facts, derived metrics, AI hypotheses and approved management decisions.
 • Make KPI formulas configurable by role and business.
 • Integrate with Finance, Sales and Operations data early because HR productivity depends on
 business context.
• Do not automate high-impact employment decisions without explicit human approval.
 • Capture the result of decisions so the system can learn what works for each business.

24. Success Definition
The HR Brain succeeds when management can answer, with evidence:
 • Do we have the right number of people?
 • Are they in the right roles and shifts?
 • What is each role expected to deliver?
 • Who or what is causing performance variance?
 • Is the problem people, process, manager, demand, inventory or target design?
 • Which management action is most likely to improve the outcome?
 • What happened after we acted?
 
Long-term objective
Right people + right role + right capacity + right cost + right management action =
stronger business outcomes.
